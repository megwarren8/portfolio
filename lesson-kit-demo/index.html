Plan, teach, and print a class from one file

The Daily Lesson Kit is the app I ran my classes from. It lays the term out on the school calendar, checks that the topics fit, and turns each class into slides, a teacher screen, and a printed handout. It is a single file that works offline. The demo opens a made-up Trigonometry class on a Tuesday early in the fall term.

Open the demo

No sign-in. Start on Home, open the day’s class, and press Present to see the slides the room sees.

Open the Lesson Kit demo

Best on a laptop or tablet. Anything you change stays in your own browser, and Reset demo puts it back.

What a teacher gets

  • The term, laid outEvery class meeting on the calendar, holidays skipped, and a pace check that says whether the topics fit before the term starts.
  • A teacher screenThe live slide, what the room sees, the next step, a lesson map, and the lesson’s own notes for each slide: what to say, what to watch for, and the answers.
  • Slides for the projectorOne idea per slide, worked examples revealed a step at a time, a timed group task with hints, and a three-question exit ticket.
  • A handout that printsGuided notes with blanks, the day’s practice problems, and an exit slip to cut off and hand in, plus a complete copy for after class.

Where the lessons come from

The demo’s two class days on the Pythagorean theorem come from the Trigonometry Lesson Studio, the ready-to-teach lessons I wrote for the course. An importer copies every sentence, number, and figure from the Studio and records where each one came from. If a single word stops matching the Studio, the check fails.

How the demo stays private

The class, its schedule, and its school are made up. The demo is its own copy of the app, built without any school data, and the site refuses to publish it if any of the school’s private details get in. It saves only to your browser, so nothing you click reaches anyone else.
